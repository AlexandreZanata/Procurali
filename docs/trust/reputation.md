# 14. Reputation

### 14.1 MVP evidence

Start with factual account age, broad recent activity, unique contact initiations received, and buyer-reported platform-attributed resolutions. Label each accurately. Exact activity timestamps need not be public; “Active recently” can mean at least one valid marketplace action in the last seven days.

Do not present contacts as successful negotiations, expose complaint counts publicly, or create a composite trust score. Raw contact totals can be inflated and are primarily operational evidence. A modest public profile may show account age and, when sufficiently supported, “Buyer-reported resolutions: 3.” It must not say “Verified sales: 3.”

### 14.2 Structured feedback

After a recorded contact, a buyer may optionally answer “Did the seller actually have the offered item?” with “Yes,” “No,” or “Unable to confirm.” Feedback opens after 24 hours and closes 14 days after the first contact; the delay reduces immediate speculative ratings and the window bounds stale-memory claims. Review completion rates and disputes before revising the window.

One feedback set is allowed per buyer/seller/request cycle. Multiple offer revisions and repeat handoffs do not create more credit. The buyer may correct the answer during the same window, with preserved revision history. A negative answer can invite an optional separate report; it is not automatically a validated report or a ban. Sellers may dispute feedback for review without seeing protected reporter details.

Use binary feedback operationally in the first MVP; broader public legitimacy summaries belong to near-term post-MVP. If such a summary is introduced, require at least five distinct reporting buyers and display the number of observations alongside the result. Five is a provisional minimum to avoid implying broad consensus from one interaction; it does not establish statistical certainty.

### 14.3 Manipulation and growth

Exclude known collusion, confirmed fabricated contacts, duplicate observations, and overturned moderation findings from derived reputation. Keep accountable correction records. Validated incidents may support an administrative restriction, but public reputation should remain factual and limited.

Future reputation can add independently verified professional identity or stronger interaction confirmation without changing the meaning of old observations. Open-ended reviews and star ratings are unnecessary for the MVP.
