# 25. Near-term post-MVP and future scope

### 25.1 Near-term post-MVP

After the core loop has useful local liquidity:

- Launch Radar Pro with one local multi-category configuration and explicit notification consent.
- Introduce commercial access intervals, renewal/cancellation rules, approved service remedies, and plan versions.
- Improve saved seller filters and lightweight retention prompts without auto-renewing buyer intent.
- Publish carefully labeled structured-feedback summaries when observation/privacy thresholds are satisfied.
- Refine limits from allowance friction and reviewed abuse rather than adding elaborate automation.
- Improve administrative prioritization and appeals based on real operational load.
- Add professional usage summaries and broader history when demand and privacy policy justify them.
- Expand to selected additional cities only where active local supply can support new demand.

### 25.2 Future capabilities

Defer services, temporary Radar passes, geographic radius/maps, advanced recommendation, sophisticated AI matching, business demand intelligence, external programmatic access, large multi-city operation, complex plan packaging, richer professional verification, and advanced reputation.

Each extension requires its own business decision: relevant units, consent, states, new abuse surfaces, metric meaning, and effect on free liquidity. A services marketplace, for example, requires service-specific availability and suitability; it is not introduced merely by adding a product category label.

Future commercial “priority” should mean defined operational convenience such as an earlier alert, subject to transparent terms. It must not deprioritize safety review, imply trust, or hide free demand. No such priority is part of the current MVP or promised first Radar plan.
